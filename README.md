## What Is the D-ZT-DS-01 Zero Trust Design v2 Exam?

The D-ZT-DS-01 exam validates your ability to design and implement Zero Trust strategies across users, devices, data, applications, networks, cloud environments, and AI-enabled environments. Dell states that candidates should understand Zero Trust principles, architectures, maturity models, implementation approaches, adoption roadmaps, and the use of visibility, analytics, automation, and orchestration to support ongoing operations.

This makes D-ZT-DS-01 a design-focused security exam rather than a narrow product exam. Candidates should be able to look at a business or technical environment, identify trust gaps, and recommend a Zero Trust architecture that reduces risk while still supporting business objectives.

## D-ZT-DS-01 Replaces D-ZT-DS-23

Dell officially retired D-ZT-DS-23 on September 10, 2026, with D-ZT-DS-01 available beginning September 11, 2026. Candidates who were still pursuing the older version had to complete scheduling and delivery before its retirement date. 

The update is not a complete rewrite of the exam. Most of the previous Zero Trust foundation remains, but the new blueprint shifts some weighting and adds a dedicated Zero Trust in AI domain.

## What Changed from D-ZT-DS-23 to D-ZT-DS-01?

The most visible update is the addition of Zero Trust in AI — 6%. At the same time, the weighting of Data Centric Zero Trust decreases from 10% to 8%, and Identity based Zero Trust decreases from 12% to 8%. The major domains such as Introduction to Zero Trust, Tenets and Pillars, Applying Zero Trust, Network Zero Trust, Cloud, Hybrid Zero Trust, and Monitoring and Maintenance remain part of the blueprint.

The biggest practical takeaway is that candidates now need to understand how Zero Trust principles extend into AI environments, not just traditional users, devices, applications, networks, data, and cloud.

## What Should You Focus on Most for the D-ZT-DS-01 Exam?

Not every D-ZT-DS-01 domain deserves the same amount of study time. The exam puts the strongest emphasis on applying Zero Trust in real environments, then builds outward into network, identity, data, AI, cloud, and ongoing monitoring.

### 1. Applying Zero Trust — Highest Priority

This is the most important domain at **22%**. Focus on Zero Trust adoption roadmaps, maturity progression, microsegmentation, Software-Defined Perimeter concepts, identity governance, and how Zero Trust applies across users, devices, applications, workloads, networks, data, and infrastructure.

### 2. Tenets, Pillars, and Continuous Monitoring

**Zero Trust Tenets and Pillars** and **Monitoring and Maintenance** each account for **14%**. Make sure you understand the core principles behind Zero Trust decisions and how visibility, analytics, automation, orchestration, and policy improvement support Zero Trust after deployment.

### 3. Network Zero Trust

At **12%**, network design is another major area. Review segmentation, least-privilege connectivity, trust boundaries, policy enforcement, and methods for reducing lateral movement.

### 4. Identity and Data Protection

**Identity Based Zero Trust** and **Data Centric Zero Trust** each represent **8%**. Focus on authentication, authorization, least privilege, data classification, access control, and protection of sensitive information.

### 5. Zero Trust in AI

Although **Zero Trust in AI** represents **6%**, it is the most important new addition in D-ZT-DS-01. Review how Zero Trust applies to AI workloads, automated services, data access, identities, monitoring, and policy enforcement.

### 6. Cloud and Hybrid Zero Trust

The cloud and hybrid domains have lower individual weights, but they are still important because many scenario questions may combine them with identity, network, and data controls.

**Recommended study order:**  
**Applying Zero Trust → Tenets & Pillars / Monitoring → Networks → Identity & Data → AI → Cloud & Hybrid**
